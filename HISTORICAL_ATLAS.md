# Historical Atlas / Ἱστορικὸς Ἄτλας

**Method.** Historical sources are not treated as one doctrine. Greek mathematical culture spans more than a millennium, crossed multiple languages and cultures, and accumulated sometimes incompatible techniques. "Greek-only" is the constraint on the *counterfactual modeling language*, not a license to rewrite the historical record.

## Philosophical origins and genuine disagreements

| Tradition | Selected texts / figures | Reconstructed question | Historical caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eleatic | Parmenides fragments; Zeno reports, especially Aristotle | What can be spoken or thought about Being? Can plurality and motion be intelligible? | Do not attribute a contemporary ban on numerical zero as a theorem of Parmenides. Zeno's arguments are transmitted by critics. |
| Early Pythagorean | Philolaus fragments; Archytas; later testimonia | What is concordance between limit and unlimited, music and proportion? | Distinguish early evidence from later legends about Pythagoras; the "music of the spheres" is not evidence of literal planetary sound. |
| Platonic | Plato, *Republic*, *Timaeus*, *Parmenides*, *Philebus* | Mathematical order, dialectic and cosmic construction | The dialogues are not laboratory laws; schools interpret them differently. |
| Aristotelian | *Posterior Analytics*, *Physics*, *Categories*, *On the Heavens* | Scientific demonstration, causes and motion | Aristotle criticizes celestial music as audible sound in *On the Heavens* II. |
| Aristoxenian | *Elements of Harmonics*, fragments | Hearing and melodic function rather than mathematical ratios alone | Does not simply reject mathematical reasoning. |

## Mathematics, measurement, and instruments

| Department | Historical anchor | Native reconstruction method | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arithmetic | Euclid, *Elements* VII–IX | Unity, multitude, divisibility, figurate relations | Euclid defines unity separately from number in VII.1–2. |
| Magnitude and proportion | Euclid, *Elements* V; Eudoxus (attribution) | Equimultiples and order comparisons | Does not need every magnitude to be commensurate. |
| Geometrical construction | Euclid I–VI, XI–XIII | Straightedge/compass propositions; stereometry | Recognize implicit existence assumptions in construction. |
| Incommensurability | Euclid X, Theaetetus tradition | Distinct kinds of magnitudes, reductio | Not evidence that Greeks knew modern set-theoretic irrationals. |
| Exhaustion and equilibrium | Archimedes, *Measurement of the Circle*, *On the Sphere and Cylinder*, *On Floating Bodies*, *On the Equilibrium of Planes* | Exhaustive bounds and centers of weight | Historical methods, not the unqualified invention of modern integral calculus. |
| Conics | Apollonius, *Conics* | Sections and constructions | The surviving corpus has transmission and translation issues. |
| Harmonics | Archytas, Aristoxenus, Nicomachus, Ptolemy *Harmonics* | String-length comparisons, interval categories | Equal string tension and other physical conditions are essential. |
| Spherical geometry | Menelaus, Ptolemy | Arcs, chords, moving celestial appearances | Competing models may fit similar observations. |
| Astronomy | Eudoxus, Aristarchus, Hipparchus, Ptolemy | Geometrical spheres and observed positions | Positional placeholders appeared in late Greek sexagesimal astronomy. |
| Applied geometry | Eratosthenes, Heron | Surveying, measurement, optical and mechanical constructions | Instruments differ across eras. |
| Number problems | Diophantus, *Arithmetica* | Determinate and indeterminate relations | Later algebraic methods are not simply Euclid's native language. |
| Commentary and systematization | Pappus, Theon, Hypatia, Proclus, Eutocius | Analysis, synthesis, edited demonstrations | Preserve differences between extant works and claims made about lost works. |

## Historiographic rules

- Describe claims about *original* texts separately from later testimonia, Byzantine manuscript copies, early modern reconstructions, and the new experimental conjectures.
- Record where originals survive only as fragments.
- Avoid attributing an undifferentiated scientific worldview to "the Greeks."
- Distinguish modern mathematical interpretation from what a historical author explicitly formulated.
- Do not treat planetary orbits as musical notes unless a measurable relation is independently justified.
- The historical archive includes exceptions to the zero-free modeling rule; the native experimental branch does not import them.

## Scope limit

This is a source-indexed historical map, not a transcription or machine-checked edition of all surviving Greek mathematics. The project will need primary-language scholarly review, dating, editions and translations to claim comprehensive philological coverage.
